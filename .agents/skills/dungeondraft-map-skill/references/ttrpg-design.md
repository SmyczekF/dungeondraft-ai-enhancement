# Encounter design and rules references

Read when designing the encounter space or assigning gameplay meaning to terrain. Apply the user's system and edition; default D&D guidance here refers to **2024**, not 2014 encounter tables.

## Authoritative references

- [Dungeon Master's Guide (2024), official contents](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024): consult chapter 3's Dungeons, Doors, Environmental Effects, Hazards and Traps, and chapter 4's encounter planning and objectives, as relevant. Access to full sections may require the user's licensed copy. Do not claim to have read inaccessible material or invent exact citations.
- [Player's Handbook (2024), official contents](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024): consult chapter 1's exploration and combat guidance and the Rules Glossary when movement, cover, visibility, climbing, jumping, falling, difficult terrain or creature size affect the map. Use relevant character or spell rules when the requested encounter depends on them. Access to full sections may require the user's licensed copy; distinguish accessible rules from assumptions.
- [Official 2024 encounter planning article](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1901-creating-combat-encounters-using-the-new-dungeon): useful for objectives and encounter difficulty. If the user requests balance or enemies, obtain party level, size and relevant constraints rather than guessing an XP budget from the artwork.
- [Official free SRD 5.2](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.pdf): an accessible rules reference when relevant DMG text is unavailable. Search Combat Encounters (printed page 202), Cover, Difficult Terrain, Climbing, Falling and Hazards as needed. It is a distinct source, not proof that every DMG section was consulted.

Verify exact mechanical rules in an accessible official source or material supplied by the user when specifying movement costs, cover benefits, hazard damage or DCs. Keep proposed terrain semantics distinct from visual decoration. Label house rules and encounter suggestions; elevation alone is not a blanket advantage rule.

## Fantasy and visual composition

For this campaign, keep the readable, moderately furnished style of the coastal maps while exploring more distinctive D&D fantasy locations. The Drowned Star Lighthouse is a successful example, not a required layout or a reason to make every encounter magical or multi-floor.

- **Give the location a recognizable focal point.** A bound-spirit lens, ship suspended in ancient roots or market inside a leviathan's ribs can organize the space. Make the defining feature readable in the overview and at token scale; surrounding rooms and routes should support its use. Avoid several equally dominant spectacles competing for attention.
- **Let fantasy affect the place.** Show how a magical mechanism, unusual inhabitant or supernatural material changes work, access, storage, defenses or escape. A lighthouse powered by a captive spirit needs a lens, controls and a reason to visit its binding chamber. Keep suggested interactions in GM notes without inventing mandatory DCs or automated rules.
- **Use a restrained material palette.** Related stone, weathered timber and water tones establish the setting; a limited accent such as cyan sea-glass distinguishes the supernatural feature. Separate walkable surfaces, walls and water through material and value contrast. Judge readability in unbaked exports so decorative glow is not carrying the floor plan.
- **Concentrate detail where activity occurs.** Group cooking equipment at the stove, charts at the planning table and supplies near unloading points. Leave useful floor space between these groups. Do not fill every gap with clutter, miniature props or evenly spaced decorations; modest furnishing can make a strong silhouette and focal point clearer.
- **Build complex features from coherent asset groups.** A podium, armature, orb and restrained rune motif can read as one arcane machine when their scales, centers and drawing order agree. Inspect the actual sprites together; avoid oversized ordinary furniture or repeated effects used only to fill space. Intentional order suits a tower or ritual apparatus while independently built structures retain the main skill's natural asymmetry.
- **Make multiple floors tell different parts of the same location.** Repeat the structural footprint and align actual stair landings, but vary each floor's function and furnishing: service, living, machinery, buried history, for example. Give players a reason to move between them. Use a contact sheet of the actual exports to review their relationship, alongside local views at playable scale; a polished concept is not the final map.

## Focused design review

Follow `enhancing` from the main skill: when enabled, include a clearly readable tactical feature by default; omit it only when it clearly does not fit the place and briefly explain why. Choose difficult terrain, cover, elevation or a suitable interaction from the actual setting; do not force height changes or every feature category into every map. When disabled, retain requested features and those needed for a coherent place.

For each important space, identify its **purpose, access, activity and relevant tactical consequence**:

- Who uses it and for what? Can occupants sleep, cook, store supplies and use structural entrances? Do not over-optimize passage around ordinary movable furniture; it normally has no VTT collision. Does a remaining roof have plausible support?
- Match supporting facilities to the site's permanence, resources and actual use. Dedicated latrines are not a default for pirate islands or rough camps; outdoor arrangements can remain off-map or implicit. Reserve formal sanitation for suitable establishments or explicit briefs, rather than treating every missing amenity as an error.
- Where does a path lead? A shortcut should arise from habitual use, terrain, maintenance or damage, rather than existing solely as a game corridor.
- Would the occupants actually build and maintain this bridge, raised deck or hatch? Check its practical purpose, support, destination and daily use. Make the function visible in the furnishings and explain unusual construction briefly in the DM notes; remove contrived features instead of explaining away every odd shape.
- What opportunities for secret rooms, concealed passages or alternate access fit this building's size, construction and use? Strongly consider them, especially in larger complexes; omit them when they have no natural place or purpose. Keep concealed access and discovery clues in DM notes. Draw secret rooms and passages on their actual playable floor and use VTT visibility plus matching concealed artwork and secret doors at every exposed entrance, including beach/exterior exits; do not create a separate secret/GM level or duplicate player/GM artwork by default.
- What can players accomplish besides defeating enemies? Consider rescue, escape, retrieval, negotiation, sabotage or discovering information when relevant to the prompt.
- Where could meaningful changes in height fit naturally? If elevation is chosen, check that it is readable, measured in feet and reachable as intended. Consider stairs, slopes, ladders and climbable surfaces. Upper floors need entrances/landings aligned with the floor below. Keep a genuinely flat location flat rather than inventing unnatural elevation.
- Is any difficult terrain justified by the conditions, and how much? It may be absent when another fitting tactical feature serves the design. Where present, does it create a meaningful constraint or choice, with distinct texture/material and clear bounds in the notes? Low rubble clusters normally slow movement without collision or sight walls. Provide a bypass when the setting supports one; an intentional bottleneck may have no route around it. Keep that limitation believable rather than adding an artificial shortcut. Use cover and visibility conditions suited to the place, with sparse or abundant cover as warranted. Keep physical cover, visibility obstruction and movement obstruction distinct.
- Does each interior with existing active lighting have at least dim illumination throughout accessible space with its doors closed? Use the user's Torch-sized minimum for map fixtures, extend dim reach or add plausible fixtures as needed, and document intentional darkness. Outdoor night is dim by campaign preference; underground darkness remains deliberate. Plan the companion export skill's core darkness regions rather than relying on daylight entering a doorway.
- What environmental interaction fits this place? Locate movable cargo, ropes, water, fire or fragile structures where someone would actually use them. Do not invent lethal mechanical effects simply because an object exists.
- Are natural hazards and ruined materials connected to the setting? Flood mud belongs near drainage or water; rubble belongs near damage; scrub grows around neglected edges. An occupied poor hut can have repaired walls and intact shelter.
- At token scale, are priorities recognizable and routes clear? Keep ordinary routes, doors and objectives readable; deliberately concealed secret entrances are an exception and should blend in from both sides.

Put heights, difficult terrain regions, suggested cover, interactions and unimplemented VTT mechanics in short DM notes. Keep player artwork free of secret labels, baked creature tokens and rules annotations unless requested.
